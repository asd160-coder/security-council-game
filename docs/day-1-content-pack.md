# Day 1 Content Pack: Cuban Missile Crisis Security Council Game

## Purpose

Day 1 introduces the crisis, teaches the player how the simulation works, differentiates the three playable roles, and establishes the first meaningful tradeoff between firmness, legitimacy, leverage, and escalation.[cite:2] The player should leave this day understanding that the crisis is serious, that perspective shapes interpretation, and that even early rhetorical choices create consequences.[cite:2]

## Day 1 learning goals

By the end of Day 1, the player should be able to:

- Explain why the discovery of missile installations in Cuba is politically and strategically alarming.[cite:2]
- Recognize that Robert Kennedy, Anatoly Dobrynin, and U Thant each approach the crisis through different priorities and pressures.[cite:2]
- Understand that public tone and private intent can differ in diplomacy.[cite:2]
- Make a first strategic choice that shapes later trust, leverage, legitimacy, and escalation.[cite:2]
- Begin constructing an evolving diplomatic response through a low-pressure drafting decision.[cite:2]

## Day 1 player experience

The emotional tone should be tense, restrained, and serious.[cite:2] The player should feel that something dangerous has begun, but the game should communicate that danger through pressure, ambiguity, and strategic framing rather than spectacle.[cite:2]

Day 1 should take about 3 to 5 minutes inside the full 20 to 25 minute scenario.[cite:2] It should be one of the easier days cognitively, since it must teach the interface and establish tone without overwhelming the player with too much text.[cite:2]

## Day 1 sequence

1. Shared crisis briefing.[cite:2]
2. Role-specific private briefing.[cite:2]
3. First formal response scene.[cite:2]
4. Tracker update and immediate consequence feedback.[cite:2]
5. Unlockable information moment.[cite:2]
6. First drafting task: tone selection.[cite:2]
7. End-of-day summary leading to Day 2.[cite:2]

## Shared crisis briefing

### Visual frame

- Background map showing the United States, Cuba, the Soviet Union, and the United Nations.[cite:2]
- One archival-style still image or reconnaissance-themed visual.
- Optional short radio/news clip with captions and skip control.[cite:2]

### On-screen briefing text

**Title:** Day 1 — Discovery

**Body copy:**

Reconnaissance has revealed missile installations in Cuba. What was once suspicion is becoming strategic reality. Across Washington, Moscow, and the United Nations, leaders are now weighing the same question: how can this threat be answered without pushing the world toward catastrophe?

Every public statement, every private message, and every signal of weakness or resolve may shape what comes next.

### Optional radio/news caption

**Suggested caption text:**
A brief radio bulletin signals rising public anxiety as governments move from uncertainty toward response.[cite:2]

## Role-specific private briefing cards

Each player sees one of the following after role selection.[cite:2] These should appear as compact cards or dossier-style panels.

### Robert Kennedy private briefing

**Role:** Attorney General of the United States

**Immediate objective:** Help shape a response that protects US credibility without triggering uncontrollable escalation.

**Pressure:** Military voices and domestic expectations may favor firmness. Delay may be read as weakness.

**Advantage:** Access to executive decision-making and the possibility of private diplomatic signaling.

**Fear:** A misstep could lead either to humiliation or to war.

**Starting unlock:** United States perspective card.

### Anatoly Dobrynin private briefing

**Role:** Soviet Ambassador to the United States

**Immediate objective:** Protect Soviet interests and strategic standing while avoiding a disastrous breakdown in communication.

**Pressure:** Public retreat would be humiliating; hardline posturing may increase danger.

**Advantage:** Ambiguity, private contact, and the ability to test the intentions of the other side.

**Fear:** Weakness may be exploited, but overreaction may trap both sides.

**Starting unlock:** Soviet Union perspective card.

### U Thant private briefing

**Role:** Secretary-General of the United Nations

**Immediate objective:** Create space for de-escalation before public positions harden beyond repair.

**Pressure:** The major powers may treat the UN as symbolic unless it acts quickly and credibly.

**Advantage:** Institutional legitimacy, neutrality, and the ability to frame restraint as a global necessity.

**Fear:** If mediation comes too late, events may outrun diplomacy.

**Starting unlock:** United Nations actor card.

## First formal response scene

### Scene setup

The player is asked to make an early public-facing choice about how to position their role in response to the discovery.[cite:2] This is not the final policy decision. It is the first signal of intent.[cite:2]

### Shared scene framing text

The world does not yet know how far this crisis will go. Officials are watching for signs of panic, weakness, resolve, and restraint. Your first words will not solve the crisis, but they will shape how others interpret your intent.

Choose your initial line carefully.

## Role-specific dialogue choices

Each role gets a tailored version of the first decision.[cite:2] The choices below are written to feel distinct while still mapping onto similar diplomatic strategies.

### Robert Kennedy choices

**Choice A — Firm warning**
The United States cannot ignore a threat of this magnitude. Any response must show that such a deployment carries serious consequences.

**Effects:** leverage +2, escalation +2, legitimacy 0, council trust -1, civilian risk +1

**Choice B — Controlled firmness**
The response must be serious, but it must also be disciplined. Strength without control may create the very disaster we seek to avoid.

**Effects:** leverage +1, escalation +1, legitimacy +1, council trust 0, civilian risk 0

**Choice C — Quiet opening for diplomacy**
Public strength matters, but so does preserving a channel for a solution. A position that leaves no room to move may leave no room for peace.

**Effects:** leverage 0, escalation 0, legitimacy +1, council trust +1, civilian risk -1

**Choice D — Legitimacy and caution**
Any response must be defensible not only strategically, but internationally. The world must see firmness joined to restraint.

**Effects:** leverage 0, escalation -1, legitimacy +2, council trust +1, civilian risk -1

### Anatoly Dobrynin choices

**Choice A — Reject alarmist framing**
The language of panic is often the language of escalation. Hasty judgments will only deepen instability.

**Effects:** leverage +1, escalation 0, legitimacy +1, council trust 0, civilian risk 0

**Choice B — Signal strength and parity**
Strategic pressure cannot be answered by public intimidation alone. Serious states respond to danger with balance, not theatrical threats.

**Effects:** leverage +2, escalation +1, legitimacy 0, council trust -1, civilian risk +1

**Choice C — Test reciprocal restraint**
If there is to be restraint, it must be mutual. One-sided demands invite resistance, but reciprocal caution may still preserve order.

**Effects:** leverage +1, escalation 0, legitimacy +1, council trust +1, civilian risk -1

**Choice D — Preserve ambiguity**
At moments like this, certainty can become a trap. It may be wiser to speak carefully until intentions are clearer.

**Effects:** leverage 0, escalation -1, legitimacy 0, council trust +1, civilian risk -1

### U Thant choices

**Choice A — Immediate call for restraint**
The first duty of states at such a moment is restraint. Escalatory rhetoric may become as dangerous as the weapons in dispute.

**Effects:** leverage 0, escalation -1, legitimacy +2, council trust +1, civilian risk -1

**Choice B — Procedure and verification**
The crisis demands more than accusation. It demands a process that can test facts, reduce suspicion, and slow the rush toward confrontation.

**Effects:** leverage 0, escalation -1, legitimacy +2, council trust +2, civilian risk -1

**Choice C — Offer mediation**
If each side fears humiliation, then diplomacy must create another path. Mediation may be the only way to preserve both peace and dignity.

**Effects:** leverage +1, escalation 0, legitimacy +1, council trust +2, civilian risk -1

**Choice D — Warn against hardened positions**
Once governments commit themselves fully in public, compromise becomes harder. The danger today lies not only in missiles, but in irreversible words.

**Effects:** leverage 0, escalation 0, legitimacy +2, council trust +1, civilian risk 0

## Immediate consequence feedback

After the player selects a choice, show a short response panel that communicates tone and likely effect without fully revealing every future consequence.[cite:2] These should be serious, compact, and readable.

### Feedback templates by strategy type

**Firm warning / strength-first feedback**
Your stance projects seriousness and resolve. Others are less likely to dismiss your position, but some now read the crisis as moving closer to confrontation.

**Controlled firmness feedback**
Your response balances pressure with caution. You have preserved authority without fully closing the door to later maneuver.

**Diplomatic opening / reciprocal restraint feedback**
Your words leave room for negotiation. Some actors may see this as wisdom; others may question whether it signals hesitation.

**Legitimacy / procedural caution feedback**
Your emphasis on process and restraint strengthens your international standing. However, not every actor will value legitimacy as highly as immediate advantage.

## Day 1 tracker guidance

Day 1 should primarily move escalation, legitimacy, and diplomatic leverage, with lighter movement on council trust and civilian risk.[cite:2] This keeps the system understandable early in the scenario.[cite:2]

Recommended baseline rule:

- Do not let any Day 1 choice create extreme outcomes on its own.
- Day 1 should establish direction, not determine the ending.
- The player's first choice should matter, but it should open pathways rather than lock them too early.

## Unlockable information moments

After the first choice, the player should gain at least one new piece of usable knowledge.[cite:2] This keeps the game loop educational and rewards attention.[cite:2]

## First drafting task: tone selection

Day 1 drafting should be low pressure and selection-based.[cite:2] The player is not asked to write yet. Instead, they choose the tone that will shape the opening line of an evolving diplomatic statement or response.[cite:2]

### Drafting prompt

Choose the tone of your opening diplomatic line.

### Drafting options

**Option 1 — Condemnatory and urgent**
This response should frame the crisis as unacceptable and requiring immediate correction.

**Option 2 — Measured but firm**
This response should acknowledge the seriousness of the threat while preserving disciplined control.

**Option 3 — Procedural and investigative**
This response should emphasize verification, process, and the need to establish a credible path forward.

**Option 4 — De-escalatory and diplomatic**
This response should reduce public temperature and protect room for future negotiation.

### Draft output behavior

The selected tone becomes the first stored fragment in the player's final diplomatic draft.[cite:2] Later days will add wording frames, revisions, and a short original student-written component.[cite:2]

## End-of-day summary screen

### Summary text

The crisis has entered a new phase. Your first response has shaped how others read your intent: as strength, caution, diplomacy, or procedural restraint. None of these paths is without cost.

Tomorrow, public pressure will grow. Private channels may open — or close. What is said next may matter even more than what has already been discovered.

## Day 1 design notes

Day 1 should not feel overloaded.[cite:2] Its job is to establish stakes, perspective, and the pattern of play.[cite:2]

What Day 1 must accomplish:

- Teach the player what kind of simulation this is.[cite:2]
- Make the three roles feel distinct immediately.[cite:2]
- Show that rhetoric has consequences.[cite:2]
- Begin the academic drafting scaffold gently.[cite:2]
- Build momentum into Day 2 without melodrama.[cite:2]
